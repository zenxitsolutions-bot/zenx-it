### The idempotency design, and a bug it caught before it shipped

Core mechanism: an occurrence's identity is `COALESCE(calls.original_scheduled_at,
calls.scheduled_at)` — the column that already, today, preserves a call's very first scheduled time
across any later reschedule. Before generating instant `D`, check whether any call for this
schedule (any status at all) or any gap already claims `D`; if so, skip. This needed no new column
on `calls` and makes an individual reschedule/cancellation permanent for free, since that call keeps
its schedule id and keeps blocking `D` forever.

**Caught a real bug in this design before writing the generator itself**: a plain "any status
claims it forever" rule breaks `regenerateFutureCalls` the moment the new pattern shares any dates
with the old one — e.g. switching a 7-day cadence to 14-day keeps every other date. Simulated it
directly (not just reasoned about): computed a 9-date weekly window, then a 5-date bi-weekly window
from the same anchor, and confirmed **all 5** bi-weekly dates collide with dates the old weekly
pattern already "claimed" via now-cancelled rows — meaning, without a fix, a regenerate under that
exact scenario would cancel the old series and then silently produce **zero** replacement calls.
Fixed by having `cancelFutureGeneratedCalls` (only ever called from the explicit regenerate path)
also detach each cancelled call (`consultation_schedule_id → NULL`) — freeing those dates back up —
while an ordinary individual cancel/reschedule via `PATCH /calls/:id` never detaches
(`updateCallSchema` has no such field at all), preserving "must not be undone by the next generation
run" for genuinely individual actions. Re-ran the simulation after the fix to confirm the freed
dates generate correctly.
